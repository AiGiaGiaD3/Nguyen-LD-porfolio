Trainer Readiness Check

A four-round self-check that makes sure every trainer delivering an onboarding session understands its design intent, not just its slides.

See the walkthrough
AudienceTrainers, before delivery (TTT)
TimeAbout 10 minutes
Bloom levelAnalyse / evaluate
FormatSolo, laptop or phone
OutputRound scores, printable
The problem

A program can be well designed and still fail in delivery. When dozens of trainers roll out the same session, some read the figures aloud, skip the reflection, or answer the learners' questions for them. Train-the-trainer sessions cover the content; they rarely check whether trainers understand why each part is there.

Design decisions
  • Teach the emotional arc first. The home screen shows the session's four stages (understand, trust, feel supported, commit) and tags each with Mind, Heart or Action.
  • Round 1 checks the real outcomes, with two plausible distractors (memorise all the figures, calculate every income item) that represent common delivery mistakes.
  • Round 2 asks for the sequence and then for the reason behind it, so trainers who remember the order but not the purpose are found out.
  • Round 3 pairs each block with its key activity and the learner output it must produce, which is what an observer would check in the room.
  • Round 4 puts the trainer in six real classroom moments, and every explanation names what is lost if the moment is handled badly.

Highlights

What makes it work

Design intent, made testable

The questions check why the session is built the way it is. That is what keeps delivery consistent when many trainers run the same program.

First attempt is kept

The result shows first-attempt and current scores side by side, so improvement is visible and the first attempt stays honest.

Explanations on demand

"Why?" panels stay closed until opened, so confident trainers move fast and unsure ones get depth.

Small details that matter on phones

Options are shuffled each session, text avoids orphan words in Vietnamese line breaks, and the action bar sticks to the bottom on small screens.
